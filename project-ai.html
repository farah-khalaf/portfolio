Hnefatafl — Viking Chess AI

A full Human-vs-Computer implementation of Hnefatafl (Tafl / "Viking Chess"), an asymmetric two-player strategy game, with a computer opponent that plans its moves using alpha-beta pruning. Built as a group project for the Artificial Intelligence course.

Alpha-beta pruning Game AI Team of 3–5

The game

Hnefatafl is asymmetric: 12 defenders and a king start in the center of the board and must get the king to any corner, while 24 attackers surround the board trying to trap him. All pieces move like a rook in chess, and pieces are captured by being sandwiched between two opposing pieces.

What the AI actually does

  • A game controller manages turn order between the human and the computer, applies each move, updates the board, and detects the end of the game — either the king escaping to a corner or being fully surrounded.
  • Board state is represented so that legal rook-style moves, captures by custodial sandwiching, and the special throne/corner capture rules can all be evaluated directly from it.
  • A utility function scores a given board state from a player's perspective, weighing piece count, the king's mobility, and proximity to escape corners.
  • The computer's moves are chosen with alpha-beta pruning — no other search algorithm — searched to a depth that scales with the selected difficulty.
  • Three difficulty levels (Easy, Medium, Hard) are implemented purely by varying that search depth.

Team execution: this was built and delivered exactly to the course's specification, split across teammates by component — board representation, the controller, move generation, the utility function, and the alpha-beta search — then integrated and demoed as a working Human vs. Computer game.
